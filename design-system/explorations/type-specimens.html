Fourteen pairings to replace SID's licensed fonts

SID sets headlines in Sirca, a light lowercase serif, and text in ABC Walter Neue; both are paid licenses. N, first, is the free twin of Pavonia. Pairings A to E are free on Google Fonts. Pairings F to J are paid, with prices and terms. K to M are free and deliberately left field. Each is set with the same Humaine copy, in the Klein & Linen palette. Pick one by letter.

  • Display light, reads well lowercase at 48–64px
  • Text clear at 13–17px, plain numerals
  • Mono labels and code
  • Data a sheet and a chart, tabular figures at 11–14px
  • Family how much it shares with Eagle Ridge

Decision · 18 Nov 2026

Card N: Fraunces Soft, with the wordmark at 800

Humaine Studio sets its name in Fraunces Heavy (800) at full softness. Headlines step down to Medium so the wordmark stays the heaviest thing on the page. Schibsted Grotesk carries the text. Recursive Mono, set half-casual, carries the labels, code and data, with corners as soft as the wordmark's. All three are free and open source.

Decided 18 Nov 2026 · the mono · free and open source

Recursive Mono at half-casual, chosen over DM Mono, Plex and JetBrains

Recursive Mono at half-casual is the choice. It is the only mono here that rounds its corners the way Fraunces Soft does, so the smallest type and the wordmark share one hand. Choose IBM Plex Mono instead if you want Humaine and Eagle Ridge tied together in the small print. Choose JetBrains Mono if field notes will carry long blocks of real code. All three use the SIL Open Font License and load free from Google Fonts.

Paid · prices read 18 Nov 2026

Buy one headline style, not a whole system

Pay only for the face the eye lands on. Canela Light from Commercial Type costs from $50 a style, once, and you own it.5 Set light and lowercase, it gives Humaine the thin voice SID gets from Sirca. Keep the free Schibsted Grotesk and DM Mono from pairing C for text and labels. That is pairing G. Body text earns little from a license; a headline earns a lot.

SID's two fonts share one teacher

Sirca is Alan Madić's re-reading of Walter Käch's 1949 serif display models.1 ABC Walter is drawn from the tool Käch built to teach students to draw letters.2 So SID's pair fits because both faces come from one hand. No free pairing above copies that. Pairing F buys it outright. The others replace it with a single foundry's own fit, or with a deliberate contrast.

How to see the real fonts here

Paid fonts cannot load from Google Fonts, so each paid specimen starts in a free stand-in, named on the card. Download the foundry's free trial files, then pick them in the Display, Text and Mono slots. The card then sets the Humaine copy in your files. The files stay in this browser tab; nothing is uploaded. Trial licenses cover testing like this, not use on a live site.

Left field · three subversions · free on Google Fonts

Break the rule the other ten obey

Every pairing above follows one formula: a light serif headline, a plain grotesk for text, a mono for code. Each of these three breaks a piece of it. K drops the pairing for one font that moves between human and machine. L trades modern for the type of Gutenberg's press. M turns the hierarchy upside down.

A manuscript with a typewriter

Blackletter was the type of Gutenberg's press and the first information revolution. Setting an AI studio in it lays claim to the second. Grenze Gotisch is the blackletter cut of Grenze, a plainer sibling that stays readable as text.17 The first letter is rubricated in Klein blue, the way scribes marked a page in red. Courier Prime adds the typewriter, the next machine in the line.

Risk. Blackletter carries baggage: heavy metal and, for some readers, Nazi Germany, though that regime banned it in 1941.18 Use it for a few words at a time.
